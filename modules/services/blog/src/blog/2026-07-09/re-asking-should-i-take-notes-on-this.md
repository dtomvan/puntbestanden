In-reply-to: https://v5.chriskrycho.com/notes/asking-should-i-take-notes-on-this/

Chris highlights that he thinks that he should take more notes on his reads.

> [...] I suspect that pausing to consider \<making more notes> will help me with being able to link people to things more easily, and more importantly to remember in more detail what I have read. I’ll learn more deeply that way, I expect.

... but I think that can spiral out of control. I know I have done it. But it depends most likely on how well-versed you are in the art of taking notes. When I discovered [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md), I turned into a _knowledge hoarder_ of sorts. My _vault_ turned into a personal wiki with everything I've ever known and everything related to it. At some point, when I read anything mildly profound, I started taking notes on it, only to never read it months later or circle back on it at all: turns out **this knowledge was never useful**, but I didn't realize it until it was too late.

One declares [Emacs bankruptcy](https://jakebox.github.io/posts/2025-08-31-bankruptcy.html), but I declared Obsidian bankruptcy!

I think I was just too obsessed with the _idea_ of taking a lot of notes and hence learning a lot more and more efficiently, but it's all a fugazi. I know it's a cliché, but "quality over quantity" really counts here. Which is why I'm sort-of scared to do that here on this brand-new blog...

All in all, I think "should I take notes on this?" amounts to "If I were to blog about this, would anyone care?". Learning in public is a widely practiced way of sharing while being mindful of what is and isn't important. But to be fair I have little doubt Chris didn't know that yet :)
